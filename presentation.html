Why this question

Every school has a predicted admit rate. Some beat it.

UC publishes an expected admit rate for each school, already adjusted for poverty (FRPM), applicant GPA, and school size. The gap between the real rate and that prediction is the story.

What we asked

For CA public high schools in 2022-2024, which school type most outperforms its expected UC freshman admit rate, after controlling for poverty, GPA, and size?

Why judges care

It surfaces the schools the model underestimates. The ones "supposed" to send the fewest students to UC are the ones quietly beating the odds.

Data & tools

Two real datasets, two tools

  • DATAdashboard_data.csv — every CA school, campus, year, with a precomputed admit_rate_residual column
  • DATAuc_freshman_admission_by_discipline.csv — campus discipline admit rates (CS context)
  • TOOLPython + pandas to filter, group, and average the residual by school type
  • TOOLStreamlit + Plotly for the live dashboard; Google Colab for the question sprint

    • Rigor check: we sum applicant/admit counts then divide. We never average percentages. Universitywide is not the sum of campuses.

    Smart AI use

    We used Gemini to stress-test the work

    Not to generate the finding, but to pressure-test it: judging our own scorecard, scanning what competitors likely built, and drafting a one-line hook.

  • Ask-the-data: Gemini answers questions using only the real numbers we computed (it cannot invent stats)
  • Judge scorecard: scores the dashboard 1-5 on question, finding, rigor, dashboard, presentation
  • Competitor radar: lists rival angles and how to differentiate
  • Wow hook: writes a punchy 25-word opener for judges

    • Key is entered in-settings, free Gemini models only, never stored.
