UC Admissions Data Challenge 2026 · Dashboard Construction

Which school type beats its
expected UC admit rate?

For CA public high schools in 2022–2024, which school type most outperforms its expected UC freshman admit rate, after controlling for poverty, applicant GPA, and school size?

A data-driven dashboard built with pandas, Streamlit, and Gemini.
Usefulness

Why this question is worth asking

  • Equity signal. Admit rates alone hide whether a school's outcomes match its profile. The residual isolates the gap.
  • Policy relevant. Shows which school types are underestimated by standard predictors, useful for counselors and districts.
  • Non-obvious. The answer flips intuition: the schools expected to send the fewest students to UC are the ones that most outperform.
  • Judge-ready. Clear time window (2022–24), population (CA public schools), and metric (residual in pp).
How we answered it

Data and tools

Data

dashboard_data.csv — school-level UC outcomes with the admit_rate_residual column (real minus expected admit rate, precomputed by organizers adjusting for FRPM poverty, GPA, and school size). Plus uc_freshman_admission_by_discipline.csv for CS context.

Stack

Python + pandas (analysis), Google Colab (notebooks), Streamlit (dashboard), Plotly (charts), Gemini (free models, AI features). GitHub for submission.

Method: filter to CA public schools, Universitywide, 2022–24; group by school type; average the residual. Sum counts then divide, never average rates. Universitywide is not the sum of campuses.

Continuation High Schools beat their expected UC admit rate by about 21.5 percentage points — the highest of any CA public school type. The schools expected to send the fewest students to UC are the ones that most outperform their predicted admit rate.
